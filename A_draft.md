## 4.1 Rivalry Among Existing Competitors: High（约 150 词）

Rivalry is the strongest force in the global EV industry. Established manufacturers such as BYD, Tesla, Volkswagen and Geely compete with fast-growing challengers such as NIO, XPeng and Leapmotor, and their products are increasingly similar. Three structural drivers intensify this rivalry. First, high fixed costs in vehicle and battery plants push firms to cut prices to keep capacity utilisation high. Second, exit barriers are high, because specialised assets and local-government support keep weaker firms in the market. Third, differentiation is narrowing, so buyers compare models mainly on price. The result is a sustained price war. In China, the average price of new energy vehicles fell by about 11.7% in 2025, more than 170 models were cut in price, and the industry's profit margin fell to a record low of 4.1% (Gasgoo, 2026). Even BYD, the market leader, reported a 19% fall in 2025 net profit (Caixin Global, 2026).

> 课件对应：竞争驱动因素（竞争者集中度、增速、固定成本、退出壁垒、差异化）

## 4.2 Threat of New Entrants: Medium（约 120 词）

Entry barriers in the EV industry are high. They include economies of scale, heavy capital requirements, access to distribution and regulatory approval. However, these barriers are not insurmountable for firms that already own brands, software capabilities and retail ecosystems. Xiaomi, for example, delivered 200,000 vehicles within 119 days of its first delivery (Electrek, 2025). By contrast, Apple abandoned its decade-long car project in 2024, which shows how costly entry remains (South China Morning Post, 2024). Combining entry and exit barriers (Lai, 2026), the EV industry sits in the "high entry, high exit" quadrant, which promises high but risky profits. Because successful entrants rarely exit, each new entrant adds capacity and intensifies rivalry further. The threat of new entrants is therefore medium.

> 课件对应：进入壁垒清单；进入/退出壁垒矩阵（Tut 3）

## 4.4 Bargaining Power of Buyers: Medium–High（约 110 词）

Individual car buyers are fragmented and buy in small volumes, which normally limits their power. However, switching costs are low, price information is transparent, and buyers can choose among many similar models. Buyer power is also rising as governments withdraw subsidies. China halved its purchase-tax exemption for new energy vehicles from January 2026, introducing a 5% tax with a reduction capped at 15,000 yuan (CarNewsChina, 2025). The United States ended its US$7,500 federal EV tax credit for vehicles acquired after 30 September 2025 (Electrive, 2025). Because cars are expensive durable goods with high price elasticity, buyers can delay purchases when incentives fall. This reinforces the downward pressure on prices described in Section 4.1.

> 课件对应：客户数量与分散程度、产品差异化；Tut 2 价格弹性（必需品 vs 奢侈品）

## 5. Threat 1: Price War and Declining Profitability（约 60 词）

High rivalry, low switching costs and the withdrawal of subsidies reinforce one another and lock firms into competing on price. Industry margins have already fallen to 4.1% (Gasgoo, 2026). Because high exit barriers prevent excess capacity from leaving the industry, this threat is structural rather than cyclical, and it will not disappear without strategic change.

## 6. Recommendation 1: Shift from Price Competition to Value Differentiation（约 100 词）

**Diagnosis:** Homogeneous products drive price-based rivalry. **Guiding policy:** Firms should increase differentiation, because greater differentiation reduces rivalry (Porter, 2008). **Coherent actions:** First, develop software-defined vehicles that earn recurring revenue through over-the-air upgrades and connected services; McKinsey & Company (2025) forecasts the automotive software and electronics market to reach US$519 billion by 2035. Second, invest in advanced driver-assistance systems as a visible point of difference. Third, separate brands so that premium sub-brands avoid mass-market price competition. Finally, support industry self-regulation, such as the 2025 pledge by 17 automakers to pay suppliers within 60 days (Associated Press, 2025).

> 课件对应：Rumelt 好战略三要素（Tut 1）；"差异化越高，竞争越低"（Tut 3）
